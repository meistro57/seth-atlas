## ITEM 1 — Bridge page: "Beyond Seth: the Missing Bridge question"

### Copy

### Why this section exists

This atlas began with one question about Seth's account of how experience becomes physical. That question turned out to be unanswerable from the material we had, and the attempt to answer it produced a different investigation — one about why the fields that study unusual experience never compare notes.

**The relationship between these two bodies of work runs one way.** Seth's mechanisms are what we set out to test. The Missing Bridge work is what happened when we tried: the constructs turned out to be unmeasurable in the available material, the claims we built on them were withdrawn one at a time, and the scope expanded because of that failure rather than in spite of it.

**Nothing in the Missing Bridge sections that follow corroborates any Seth mechanism.** No experiment reported here supports Seth's account of reality, and the absence of a refutation is not support. A reader who scrolls from the Reality Engine into these pages should understand that the direction of inference is the reverse: Seth is the premise, not the conclusion.

### Use-of-language rules (editorial, not rendered)

Three things this page and every page after it must never do:

1. **Never present Missing Bridge findings as support for Seth's mechanisms.** The correct phrasing is "Seth proposes X; we attempted to test X; the attempt produced methodological results."
2. **Never present the §5 protocol as a study.** It is a proposal with unresolved feasibility. The word "study" is reserved for work that was run.
3. **Never present a null result as evidence against any generating mechanism.** A finding that a coding scheme detected no structure means the scheme detected nothing, not that nothing exists.

## ITEM 2 — The three-level distinction

### Copy

### Three things that get called the same thing

Almost every claim that disparate phenomena share a cause collapses three separate questions into one. They are kept apart throughout this site.

**Shared mechanism** — one causal process produces several classes of experience. This is the strong claim, and it is the one that requires a generator to be identified, measured and shown to produce both classes.

**Shared trigger** — one variable raises the rate of experiences across classes without causing any of them. Bad sleep, stress, and geomagnetic conditions are candidates. A shared trigger is not evidence of a shared phenomenon. It is evidence of a shared *observation apparatus* — human nervous systems in a variable world — and it points away from unification rather than toward it.

**Shared interpretation** — one narrative template explains varied experiences after the fact. The experience may be constant while the label drifts: demon, fairy, alien. This is the weakest claim about reality and the easiest to demonstrate, because it only requires reports to be compared, not phenomena.

Most of what reads as evidence for unification in this literature is the third level presented as the first. That distinction is the single most useful thing this investigation produced, and it is the reason the later sections describe failures rather than discoveries.

## ITEM 3 — Phase summaries (research.json entries)

Each entry ends in what it failed to establish, per Ohm's requirement. Status fields use the existing vocabulary plus one new value, `unexecuted`, agreed as a structural necessity.

### P2 — Phase II: sleep paralysis and the first real evidence

**Claim:** Sleep paralysis is the one domain in this literature where "one process, many labels" is demonstrated on real data. The physiological mechanism — REM atonia persisting into wakefulness — is established, and the hallucinated content varies with local culture while the mechanism stays fixed.

**Status:** `solid`

**Limitation:** this demonstrates shared mechanism **within the sleep-paralysis class**. It does not extend to hauntings, poltergeists or entity contacts. Extending it is the category error the investigation exists to catch.

### P2a — Phase II audit: six corrections

**Claim:** An evidence audit of Phase II produced six corrections, all running in the same direction — claims asserted slightly past what the retrieval supported.

**Status:** `explicit`

**Limitation:** the two most serious were grading source authenticity and event reliability under a single label, and claiming a negative result for UFOs and poltergeists that the searches performed could not support.

### P3 — Phase III: the poltergeist census

**Claim:** Focal-person localisation is the majority pattern in published poltergeist case reports, with a stated denominator of 906 coded cases rather than a generalisation from two famous ones.

**Status:** `provisional`

**Limitation:** the citation list behind it was selected to *illustrate phenomenon categories*, not to sample them, and the author's own appendix shows the skew — 60% of selected cases under a century old against 43% in his own database. A majority pattern in the literature is not a majority pattern in the world. The stress variable, the mechanism's supposed driver, is undocumented in every accessible case.

### P4 — Phase IV: AWARE-II and remote viewing

**Claim:** AWARE-II observed zero correct identifications of the hidden visual image among 28 interviewed survivors; the authors report the implicit test was underpowered and the subgroup with usable target exposure is not established.

**Status:** `solid` (the observation); `provisional` (its interpretation)

**Limitation:** the observation is a null on the explicit channel over a small denominator, and it excludes a large effect rather than ruling out a small one. The remote-viewing comparison is incomplete: the AIR review found the effects statistically real but unattributable, because the same viewer, judge and target were reused across sessions. The claim that the two fields differ in *falsifiability structure* is a hypothesis about this one comparison, not a law about two disciplines.

### P5 — Phase V: apparitions and entity encounters

**Claim:** The strongest supported connection is between unusual-experience *reporting* and interpretation processes.

**Status:** `provisional`

**Limitation:** the cross-literature comparison originally designed for this phase was **withdrawn** — it had no discriminating power, because either result was already predicted before data collection. The replacement is within-population and unrun. And the surviving claim, while measurable, **constrains no hypothesis and rules out nothing**; it describes reporting, not experience.

### What we got wrong, and when

This page exists because a research site that documents its own error trail is worth more than one that reads as a sequence of successes. Every entry is dated and nothing has been deleted.

**The pattern worth noticing:** across five phases the same failure recurred, always in one direction — a claim asserted just past what the evidence gathering had supported. It never failed in the other direction; we never under-claimed in a way that cost us a finding. That asymmetry is more instructive than any individual retraction.

## ITEM 5 — The unexecuted protocol (research.json entry, status `unexecuted`)

### Copy

### A proposed comparison — not run

**This is a proposal. No data were collected, no participant was recruited, and no result exists.**

The question: do free sensory descriptions of unusual experiences cluster by the reporter's own interpretation, or by characteristics of the episode independent of that interpretation?

The design: within one population only — people reporting sleep paralysis. Sensory description is collected first, as separate modality questions (what was seen, what was heard, what was physically felt), with no invitation to characterise the episode and none of the category words available. Interpretation is collected only afterwards. Prior exposure is recorded as a covariate.

The measure: whether a coder blind to the stated interpretation can recover it from the sensory description better than chance. **Chance is 1/6 ≈ 16.7%** across six interpretation categories. At **n = 120** analysable descriptions (recruited to 200), a two-tailed binomial test at α = 0.05 detects a true recovery rate of **at least 27%** with power ≈ 0.80.

The decision rule fixed in advance: **a null may only be declared if the recruitment target was met.** If it was not, the result is uninterpretable and will be reported as such — never as evidence that nothing exists.

**Feasibility is unresolved.** Recruitment pathway, consent handling, and an inter-rater-reliability check are all outstanding, and **no coded cross-cultural sleep-paralysis baseline is currently available to us** — a statement about our holdings, not about the literature. Such baselines very likely exist elsewhere. Whether this study can be executed at all is an open question, and until it is answered the entry stays in this state.

## ITEM 6 — Hope's C3 Nostradamus pilot (separately attributed)

### Copy

### A separate preliminary investigation

**Conducted independently by Hope, in her own environment, and reported here without integration into this site's causal model.**

What was tested: 14 Nostradamus quatrains, assessed against historical events under provisional scoring criteria developed for the exercise.

**What the strength of this result is:** 14 claims, self-developed provisional scoring, a methodology that remains under review, and two dated outcomes still awaiting independent historical confirmation. The analysis also identified multiple publication layers, later historical reinterpretations, and a known source of prior translations — all of which complicate any claim that a text predicted an event.

**Under those provisional criteria**, no novel-information hit was independently verified.

**That is a property of 14 claims under preliminary scoring. It is not a verdict on channeling, and it is not evidence for or against any Seth mechanism.** The two investigations ask different questions by different methods, and a null here leaves Seth's account exactly where it was: untested.
