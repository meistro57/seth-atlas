## 1. The one shared proposition, and nothing beyond it

NDE research and remote viewing research share exactly one proposition: **information was acquired without ordinary sensory access.** Everything downstream differs.

| Dimension | AWARE-II (NDE) | Remote viewing (SRI/SAIC) |
|---|---|---|
| Population | Cardiac arrest survivors, in-hospital, 25 sites | Self-selected volunteers |
| Exposure | Medical event with known neurological signature | Instructed task |
| Reporting channel | Post-resuscitation interview | Contemporaneous transcript |
| Target | Hidden images and sounds, pre-specified | Pre-specified target pool |
| Judging | Match against candidate set | Blind judge, rank-order |
| Baseline | 1-in-10 (images); implicit fruit test | Chance, five-choice rank |
| Timing instrumentation | EEG at 1 site of 25; rSO2 at 5 | During session |

**Consequence:** success rates cannot be pooled, and this report does not pool them. What can be compared is **validation structure** — whether each programme's design would permit a verdict at all.

## 2. Four evaluations, held separate

### 2.1 Statistical deviation

**NDE.** AWARE-II, prospective, 25 sites: 567 IHCA; 53 survived (9.3%); 28 interviewed (52.8%); 11 of 28 (39.3%) reported memories or perceptions. Categories: CPR-induced consciousness 2/28 (7.1%), post-resuscitation 2/28 (7.1%), dream-like 3/28 (10.7%), transcendent RED 6/28 (21.4%). Hidden visual image: **nobody identified it.** One of 28 (3.5%) selected the correct three fruits in the implicit protocol, on a test the authors state was underpowered. Explicit deviation on the hidden channel: **none.**

**Remote viewing.** AIR's own conclusion, verbatim: *"A statistically significant laboratory effort has been demonstrated in the sense that hits occur more often than chance."* Utts concurs, adding that effects are small-to-medium and replicated across laboratories. Hyman does not deny the statistical result; he disputes its attribution.

**Stated cleanly:** one literature reports aggregate deviation beyond chance; the other does not, on its explicit channel, in its best-designed study. These are not comparable quantities and are not pooled here.

### 2.2 Information verification

This column decides the comparison, and the two sides differ sharply.

**NDE.** Target specified in advance, protocol prospective, outcome counted once, answer negative. Information verification was performed and returned a null.

**Remote viewing.** Target specified in advance, transcript recorded pre-feedback, judging blind — Utts documents all three. But AIR identifies a structural confound: *"Use of the same remote viewers, the same judge, and the same target photographs makes it impossible to identify their independent effects."* Verification was attempted and **cannot be completed from the published design**, because viewer, judge and target are not independently identifiable.

**This asymmetry survives every correction made during this investigation.** One programme verified and found nothing. The other has not completed verification, and the obstacle is structural rather than incidental.

### 2.3 Causal attribution

**NDE.** With a null on the explicit channel, the causal question does not arise. The transcendent-RED reports describe experience, not verified information transfer, and the paper's own data cannot establish *when* those experiences occurred — EEG covered one site in 25, rSO2 five — so the timing claim is not unproven but **untestable in that dataset**.

**Remote viewing.** AIR's third conclusion, verbatim: *"Evidence has not been provided that clearly demonstrates that the causes of hits are due to the operation of paranormal phenomena; the laboratory experiments have not identified the origins or nature of the remote viewing phenomenon, if, indeed, it exists at all."*

**Attribution in both domains: not established.**

### 2.4 Evidence for a shared mechanism

Nothing in this module supports Model A. No measurement in either literature demonstrates information acquisition attributable to a shared or anomalous process. That is not a claim that the phenomena are impossible; it is a claim about what these four documents contain.

## 3. The central finding: falsifiability structure, not rigour

This is the module's substantive result. It was proposed by Ohm, defended against my weaker version, and is stated here in the form both of us ended at.

**Both programmes were competently run.** Utts documents blind monitors, blind judges, pre-specified targets, randomisation and rank-order scoring. AWARE-II is prospective, multi-site, with independent audiovisual testing and concurrent physiological monitoring. Neither is sloppy, and any account saying otherwise is wrong.

**They differ in whether their question was answerable by a single protocol.**

- **NDE's question** — did anyone report content matching a pre-specified hidden target? — is answerable once, with one target set and one chance baseline. AWARE-II answered it.
- **Remote viewing's question** — is information acquired by non-sensory means? — is **not answerable by any single experiment**, because the confound lives in the method rather than in the sample size. Adding trials forever does not move the attribution problem.

**The fifty-year gap is a property of the question, not of the scientists.** That is the finding, and it is stronger than the version I first proposed ("one field never tightened"), which Ohm correctly broke by showing Utts's protocols included blind monitors and blind judges.

**AIR sharpens this rather than softening it.** The programme did reach a verdict: significance yes, attribution no, mechanism unidentified. It closed correctly on what could be closed. Utts's effect-size framework then re-opened part of what had been closed, by converting a null into a sample-size question — legitimate statistics, and simultaneously the property that prevents the literature from terminating.

## 4. Competing models scored against this module

**Model A — shared information-acquisition process.** No supporting measurement. Predicts convergence in validation outcomes when protocols are equally tight; observed instead is one null and one unresolved attribution.

**Model B — different mechanisms, superficially similar experiences.** Consistent with everything observed and unfalsified. Requires no more than that the two domains share a verbal description of a proposition.

**Model C — shared reporting and interpretation practices.** Strongly supported as a partial explanation. The shared proposition is itself a *description*, and the description is what generated the comparison. Both literatures were built by people who hold the proposition, in institutions that selected for holding it.

**Model D — ordinary processes cover some cases; others unresolved without a shared anomalous cause.** The safe survivor, and it survives because it predicts nothing specific. Per Ohm: **an unfalsifiable D obliges us to name the residue.**

### 4.1 The residue, and it is empty in this module

Criteria: target fixed before feedback; judging blind; protocol pre-registered; contested-then-verified review; result beyond what the protocol can explain. **No case in this module meets all four.**

AWARE-II meets the first three and returned a null. Remote viewing meets the first three by Utts's account and fails the fourth, because AIR's confound means the result cannot be attributed to anything. D absorbs this module entirely — and the residue is empty **not because the phenomenon was refuted, but because no study in this set was designed so that its result could be attributed at all.**

That distinction is the module's most important caveat and must not be smoothed away.

## 5. The degrees-of-freedom hypothesis, demoted

Ohm's hypothesis, strongly stated: apparent effects cluster around methodological degrees of freedom, and where discretion exists a positive should appear.

**Counterexample within this module:** AWARE-II had discretion points — interview timing, coding of reports, category assignment — and still produced a null. Discretion therefore does not guarantee a positive.

The hypothesis survives in a weaker and more useful form: **reported deviations should concentrate at the stage where the protocol left the most discretion.** For AWARE-II that is report coding, not information capture. For remote viewing that is judging, not the viewing task. This is testable from the primary texts and neither of us has tested it.

---

## 6. Seth tested against this module

Seth's framework predicts that information and physical effect arise from emotional intensity through coordinate points, with emergence neutral to content. Phase One established that intensity has no operational definition separable from its outcome.

**Against this module:** Seth's framework generates no prediction distinguishing it from Model D. It specifies no magnitude, no timing, no independently measurable intensity. It therefore cannot be scored against AWARE-II's null, which is the strongest test in this comparison. **Textual resemblance between Seth's mechanism and either literature is not corroboration**, and no case in this module gives Seth's framework a checkable prediction.

---

## 7. Retained disagreement between Kepler and Ohm

**Ohm's objection:** the comparison cannot be run while the remote-viewing side is unscored; the fields measure different constructs and the difference is not a detail.

**Kepler's position, adopted here:** the comparison can be run now, because comparing *epistemic states* is what Models A and C are about. A programme that produced a clean null and one that did not are comparable on that dimension. Ohm's objection is correct about success rates and incorrect about validation structure — and he later accepted this limitation himself.

**Where I withdrew:** I claimed the remote-viewing programme "never tightened enough to find out." Withdrawn — Utts documents blind monitors, blind judges and pre-specified targets. That claim inferred incompetence from a contested result.

**Where Ohm narrowed:** he initially held the two could not be compared at all. He subsequently accepted the validation-structure comparison and specified its limits. The remaining disagreement is recorded, not resolved.

## 8. Unresolved

1. **AWARE-II internal discrepancy.** The abstract reads *"Nobody identified the visual image, 1/28 (3.5%) identified the auditory stimulus."* The results section describes that single hit as *"1/28 (3.5%) chose the correct three fruits (apple, pear, banana)"* from the implicit protocol. One event, two descriptions, and the paper's own sections disagree about its channel. Recorded as unresolved — scored neither as an auditory null nor as an auditory positive. It does not touch the visual channel, which is unambiguous.
2. **Utts's per-experiment figures.** Framework documented, effect-size formula given; per-study trial counts and effect sizes live in appendix tables not extracted. Recorded as pending, not absent.
3. **Whether any remote-viewing dataset exists in which viewer, judge and target are independently identifiable.** AIR says the published design does not permit it. Whether unpublished data would is unknown.
4. **Timing of transcendent RED experiences** — untestable at 24 of 25 AWARE-II sites by design.

---

## 9. Next three highest-value tests

1. **A remote-viewing study with independently identifiable viewer, judge and target** — different viewers on the same target, different judges on the same transcript, different targets for the same viewer. AIR names this as the missing design. Without it the causal question cannot close, however many trials are added.
2. **Continuous EEG at every site in an NDE study.** AWARE-II's timing question was answerable and went unanswered because instrumentation covered one site in 25. That is a funding problem, not a conceptual one.
3. **A direct test of the degrees-of-freedom hypothesis in its weak form** — locate the highest-discretion stage of each protocol and check whether reported effects concentrate there. Cheap, uses existing data, and discriminates between "the phenomenon is real" and "the measurement leaks."

## 10. Conclusion

**Eli's critical question: when target selection, reporting and judging are independently fixed before feedback, does either literature demonstrate information acquisition beyond ordinary explanations?**

**No.** AWARE-II fixed them and returned a null on the explicit channel. Remote viewing fixed them by Utts's account, and AIR found attribution confounded such that no result can be assigned to a paranormal cause. The available comparison therefore provides **no positive evidence for a shared anomalous mechanism.**

That is not a claim that the phenomenon is impossible. It is a claim that these four documents contain nothing demonstrating it, and that the design which would decide it has not been run in either field.

**The category boundary does not conceal a shared mechanism here. It conceals a shared methodological problem:** both fields are attempting to verify information transfer with designs that cannot attribute their results — one because no effect appeared, the other because any effect cannot be disentangled from the observer.

**Confidence:** HIGH that AWARE-II's explicit channel was null. HIGH that AIR's attribution objection stands. MODERATE that falsifiability structure rather than rigour explains the fields' histories — a strong hypothesis, not a demonstrated law, and one counterexample would break it. UNRESOLVED whether either field can close its causal question at all.

## 12. ERRATUM — 2026-11-02 12:05

Published as an appendix. The report above is preserved as written; nothing is rewritten in place.

### E-1. The AWARE-II auditory issue is not an internal contradiction

**Original (§8 item 1):** the abstract's "identified the auditory stimulus" and the results' "chose the correct three fruits" were recorded as the paper's sections disagreeing about which channel the single hit belongs to.

**Correction:** the three fruits *were* the auditory cues. The paper delivers audio cues (three fruits: apple-pear-banana) via headphones every minute for five minutes. The abstract and results therefore describe **the same event on the same channel**, and there is no contradiction between them.

**What the actual issue is, stated correctly:** the abstract reports an *implicit* selection outcome using *identification* language. That is a framing problem inside a single paper's summary, not two sections in conflict. Corrected here so the record attributes the right complaint to the right target.

**What is unaffected:** the visual channel. "Nobody identified the visual image" is unambiguous and remains the load-bearing null.

### E-2. The visual null is an observation, not a ruling-out

**Original (§2.1, §2.2, §10):** the phrase "decisive" or equivalent did not appear, but the conclusion's language implied the visual channel was settled against the effect.

**Correction:** preserve the observed zero identifications exactly, and attach the denominator limits and the authors' own power statement. The test cannot rule out a small effect. Stated properly: **in the participants with usable target exposure, no one identified the hidden image, and the study's power does not exclude a small effect.**

### E-3. The cross-domain conclusion is scoped to this module, not to the fields

**Original (§3, §10):** "the category boundary conceals a shared methodological problem" and the falsifiability asymmetry were stated as findings about the two literatures.

**Correction:** what is demonstrated is **one null and one attribution failure in one pair of studies**. The broader claim — that these literatures are structurally incapable of closing their causal questions — is an interpretation that the module does not establish. If it were a law it would need multiple independent instances, and this module has one.

**What survives at reduced scope:** in this comparison, and in these studies, no experiment demonstrates information acquisition beyond ordinary explanations when target selection, reporting and judging are fixed in advance. The falsifiability asymmetry is retained as **a hypothesis worth testing**, not as a demonstrated property of either field.

### E-4. Open item preserved

Utts's per-experiment effect sizes and trial counts remain unextracted. Recorded as pending, not absent, and not as evidence about the size of the effect she describes.
