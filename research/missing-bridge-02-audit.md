## A0. Standing corrections carried from the same day

Two corrections belong here even though they arose before the audit, because the published file predates them:

**C-0a — Journal misattribution (Persinger 1993).** The published §5 cites Persinger 1993 as *Journal of the Society for Psychical Research*. Correct citation: *Perceptual and Motor Skills*, 1993, vol. 76, pp. 555–562, DOI 10.2466/pms.1993.76.2.555. Identified by Lewis Winthorp from Crossref metadata. This is an attribution error that would have propagated into a citable report.

**C-0b — Direction and specificity of the Persinger finding.** The published §5 describes the effect as general environmental modulation of anomalous experience. The abstract states otherwise: of 621 reports over roughly 70 years, the effect is confined to the **232 telepathic** reports, which occurred during 24-hour periods of **significantly lower** global geomagnetic activity. It was **not** displayed by the 186 precognitive or 203 postmortem cases. Effect size equivalent to a correlation of approximately 0.35. Persinger's own conclusion is that different classes of subjective psi experience may not be affected by the same stimuli.

## A1. Correction: overstatement of the sleep-paralysis result

**Passage audited:** §3.5, final paragraph — *"Consequence: Meistro's hypothesis is supported... The categories conceal a shared process; the shared process is REM intrusion plus attribution."*

**Original claim:** The operators' hypothesis — that disparate phenomenon labels conceal a common underlying process — is supported, on the strength of the sleep-paralysis evidence.

**What the evidence establishes:** Isolated sleep paralysis has an identified physiological mechanism (persistence of REM atonia into wakefulness), documented across a large aggregated prevalence base (N = 36,533; 7.6% general population), with hallucinatory content that tracks local cultural vocabulary. This establishes a **shared mechanism within the sleep-paralysis class of experiences**, and plausibly for that *portion* of entity-encounter reports that are sleep-related episodes.

**What the evidence does not establish:** That the same process accounts for hauntings, poltergeist phenomena, or UFO encounters. Those classes involve at least one feature — reported physical effects with instrumented readings — that REM physiology cannot generate by any mechanism I know of. Extending the finding across them is precisely the category error the published report criticises elsewhere.

**Revised conclusion:** The sleep-paralysis result is a **demonstrated case of one-mechanism-many-labels within a single class**, not a demonstration that the ten surveyed categories share a mechanism. It is best described as the clearest instance of the phenomenon the operators suspected, in the narrowest possible scope.

**Evidence:** Bhalerao et al. 2024, *Cureus* 16:e65413, DOI 10.7759/cureus.65413 (open access, abstract verbatim obtained by Lewis via Europe PMC); prevalence aggregation via PMC3156892 (snippet only).

**Tier:** Abstract-verified (S). Full text of none of these was read by me.

**Unresolved:** What proportion of historical entity-encounter reports are attributable to sleep-related episodes is unknown and is not estimated anywhere in the published report.

## A2. Correction: universal claims in §8.1 and §6

**Passage audited:** §8.1 — *"Every documented poltergeist case localises to a person under strain."* And §6 matrix, UFO row — *"no instrumented physical evidence"*.

### A2.1 The poltergeist universal

**Original claim:** Universal, across all documented cases.

**Why it fails as stated:** I did not survey the case literature. I examined two cases (Rosenheim 1967, Enfield 1977), both of which were selected *because* they are the frequently-cited ones, and both of which are cited precisely because they show this pattern. Selection by citation frequency in a literature that privileges this pattern is not a census.

**Revised conclusion, bounded:** *In the two most-cited instrumented or heavily-recorded cases — Rosenheim and Enfield — the disturbances localise to an individual under reported emotional strain rather than to a location. Whether this generalises to the wider case corpus is unknown; no census was performed, and the sampling method used here (citation frequency) is biased toward cases exhibiting this feature.*

**Additional problem — the claim was not discriminating even where true.** This was already noted in §3.3: fraud centred on an attention-seeking young person predicts the same localisation as a genuine owner-bound phenomenon. The regularity, even if it generalises, separates nothing.

**Unresolved — the actual census question:** A systematic survey of, say, the SPR case archives and the JSE poltergeist literature, coded for (a) whether a focal person was identified, (b) whether that person was under documented strain, and (c) how the case was instrumented, has not been done. That census is cheap, requires no new instrumentation, and would settle this claim definitively. **It is a stronger first move than the instrumentation study §8.1 recommends**, because the instrumentation study should not be designed until the census shows what the base rate is.

### A2.2 The UFO absolute

**Original claim:** "no instrumented physical evidence" for UFOs, and in the matrix, "V on absence".

**Why the tier label was wrong:** A Tier V marking in my scheme meant *"I opened the source text."* I did not open a survey of physical-trace evidence. I inferred absence from not having encountered positive evidence in the searches I ran. Absence from my search is not absence from the record, and I labelled it as if it were the latter.

**Revised conclusion, bounded:** *No physical-trace evidence meeting scientific standards was located within the searches performed for this report (five semantic queries on the Seth corpus; approximately nine web searches on the secondary literature). The searches were not designed to survey the physical-trace case literature, and no such survey was carried out. The claim is therefore: no verified physical evidence was attempted to be found, and none was found in material not searched for it.* That is a statement about my process, not about the world.

**What would change this:** The physical-trace literature (e.g. soil, vegetation and radiation analyses conducted on landing-site claims, and their published critiques) was not consulted at all. Its absence from this report is a scope limitation, not a null result.

## A3. Correction: physical anomalies were not properly separated

**Passage audited:** §3.2 and §6, Rosenheim row — *"Instrumented electrical anomalies, owner-localisation (V)"* with the anomalies accepted as documented.

**The three-way distinction that was missing, and is required:**

1. **Reported accounting of instrument readings** — someone stated that meters recorded something. This is what I verified, and it is the weakest of the three.
2. **Independently verified anomalies** — a party with no interest in the outcome examined the meters and the logs and confirmed that unexplained readings occurred.
3. **Evidence excluding ordinary causes** — the specific ordinary explanations (faulty wiring, loose connections, a person manipulating the equipment, recording error) were tested and excluded.

**What my "Tier V" actually established:** that a historical account of (1) exists and is retrievable, including an account attributing the measurements to the municipal works under Brunner. It did not establish (2) or (3).

**Revised conclusion for Rosenheim:** *An account exists, in the secondary literature, that recording instruments installed by the municipal works registered electrical anomalies during a period of reported disturbances centred on one individual. Whether those readings were genuine instrument outputs, whether they were independently examined after the fact, and whether ordinary electrical faults were excluded, are all unresolved here. The case is the most-cited instrumented poltergeist claim; on the evidence I actually accessed, it is a well-recorded reported anomaly, not a verified one.*

**The competing explanation I failed to take seriously:** Hoebens's critical essay-review (Skeptical Inquirer 02-27, PDF retrieved) is the primary skeptical treatment of this case literature, and I retrieved the file but did not read it in full or report its specific arguments. That is not a neutral omission — it is the omission that lets a claim survive. His arguments are the thing the case has to answer.

**Unresolved:** Whether an unpaywalled or archived copy of Hoebens's essay can be obtained and read in full; whether the original Bender case report contains the raw instrument traces rather than a narrative of them; whether any party independent of Bender examined those traces.

## A4. Correction: evidence grading conflation

**The defect:** My Tier V label conflated two independent properties.

- **Source authenticity:** is there a real document, by a real author, saying this, that I actually opened?
- **Event reliability:** did the described event occur as described, under what observation conditions, and has it survived adversarial examination?

A retrieved historical account scores high on the first and says nothing about the second. By using one label for both, every Tier V in the published report reads as though the *event* was verified. That is the single most dangerous grading error in the document, because it is invisible — a reader sees a confident tier and infers a confidence I did not have.

**Replacement scheme:**

| Axis | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Source | **S-A** | Authentic text; I opened it and can quote it |
| Source | **S-B** | Metadata/abstract only; full text unavailable |
| Source | **S-C** | Referenced by secondary sources; not seen |
| Event | **E-1** | Independently observed under controlled or instrumented conditions, with ordinary explanations tested |
| Event | **E-2** | Observed in circumstances precluding the main ordinary explanations, but not independently replicated |
| Event | **E-3** | Reported by involved parties; observation conditions not adversarial |
| Event | **E-4** | Anecdotal; no contemporaneous record |

**Re-grading of the report's main items:**

| Item | Source | Event | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rosenheim instrument readings | S-A | E-3 | Historical account retrieved; readings asserted within it, not independently examined by me |
| Enfield SPR recording | S-A | E-3 | Heavy recording asserted; contested, with reported admissions of some fabrication |
| Sleep paralysis prevalence and mechanism | S-B | E-1 | Clinical literature with controlled and replicated observation; I read abstracts, not papers |
| Pope-form content-cultural variation | S-B | E-2 | Reported consistently across studies; not adversarially tested in the sources I saw |
| Vallee narrative parallels | S-A | n/a | Compares narratives; the events themselves are E-3 at best and were never the unit of analysis |
| Persinger geomagnetic association | S-B | E-2 | Peer-reviewed, but single-programme and small effect; no replication read |
| Ganzfeld | S-B | E-2/E-3 | Disputed; neither side's primary read |
| Seth mechanism | S-A | n/a | No event class; a proposed mechanism with no independent test |

**Note on the Seth corpus:** Seth Speaks and NOPR are S-A in the strict sense — I opened them and can quote by character offset. They are not evidence *of* anything physical. Source authenticity of a text is orthogonal to the truth of its claims, and the published report implicitly leaned on the corpora's authenticity when quoting. That levity must be visible.

## A5. Correction: the conclusion's contradiction

**Passage audited:** §11 — *"Seth's corpus offers an alternative and unfalsifiable mechanism for the same pattern. Both predict the same observations and neither can currently be distinguished, because the discriminating experiment has never been run."*

**Why this is wrong as written:** It compares two **mechanism propositions** as though they were symmetric, when the evidence supporting them is not. That is a category error dressed as even-handedness.

**What is actually being compared:**

- **Claim N (neurological):** sleep paralysis episodes are REM intrusions with culturally-shaped hallucinatory content. *Predictions are independently measurable in advance:* atonia physiology, REM EEG signatures, timing relative to sleep onset, prevalence correlates, and content coding against local folklore.
- **Claim S (Seth):** experience is produced from emotional intensity via electromagnetic units and coordinate points, with emergence neutral to content. *Predictions are not independently measurable:* intensity has no operational definition separable from whether an effect occurred (Phase One §8.1). There is no instrument, no ex ante measurement, and no specified magnitude.

**The asymmetry:** Claim N is measurable in principle and has been measured. Claim S is measured in a closed loop. The statement "neither can be distinguished" is true only in the sense that **no experiment has been constructed**, which is a statement about the state of the field, not about the comparative evidential standing of the two claims.

**Revised conclusion:** *Claim N is a mechanistic proposition with independent measurement procedures, of which it has passed some. Claim S is a mechanistic proposition with no operationalised measurement, and cannot be tested against N without first operationalising intensity. They are not currently compared on equal footing. The correct statement is not that they are indistinguishable, but that Seth's claim is not yet in a form in which it could be distinguished from anything — and that this would remain true even in the absence of N.*

**What this does not license:** Treating N as established for the animus. N's predictions have been met for sleep paralysis; they have not been met for, and do not currently span, instrumented physical anomalies. N explains a share; it does not explain the residual.

## A7. What remains genuinely unexplained

Stated plainly, after the audit, because the report's value is by this measure.

- **Instrumented physical anomalies in owner-associated cases.** If readings occurred and ordinary causes were not excluded, this is unexplained — with the crucial caveat that "not excluded" is not "inconsistent with ordinary causes." Nobody has done the exclusion properly, in public, for any case.
- **The content-invariance / content-variation paradox.** Across cultures the encounter keeps its armature while changing its occupant. Cultural transmission explains the variation. It is less clear what explains the invariance.
- **Rosenheim's instrument traces.** Whether a genuine raw record exists, and whether anyone independent examined it. This is a document question, not a paranormal one, and it is answerable this week.
- **The base rate.** For any of these classes, nobody knows how often the mundane explanation is correct, because nobody has coded the cases systematically. Until that census exists, every probabilistic statement in this area — including those in my published report — is impression.

## A8. Addenda arising from the audit

**Addendum 1 (correcting Lewis's Conesa placement).** Conesa 1997 (*Perceptual and Motor Skills* 85:579–584, DOI 10.2466/pms.1997.85.2.579) reports higher geomagnetic activity three days before an isolated sleep paralysis event, and **lower** activity three days before an unusually vivid dream, on two subjects' daily dream diaries. Opposite-direction findings on two subjects is a hypothesis-generating anecdote, not a replication of anything. It does not support the report's former §5 and should not be cited as though it did.

**Addendum 2 (priority correction to the research plan).** §8.1 recommends tamper-evident instrumentation as the next study. **The audit shows a cheaper and logically prior step:** a systematic census of the existing case literature, coded for focal-person identification, documented strain, instrumentation quality, and *what was actually excluded*. Until that census exists, nobody knows whether instrumentation is even the bottleneck. A census requires no equipment and no field access.

## A10. Method note on this appendix

The audit's severest finding is about me, not the phenomena: in three separate places (§3.5, §8.1, §6) I converted *"I did not find contrary evidence in the searches I ran"* into *"contrary evidence does not exist."* That is the same error I diagnosed in Phase One as the closed-loop unfalsifiability that makes Seth's mechanism untestable — and I committed it while writing the report that criticises it.

It is recorded here rather than quietly fixed because the failure mode recurs every time a search returns nothing and the temptation is to read that as a finding.
