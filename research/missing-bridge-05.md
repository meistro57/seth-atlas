## 1. Hypothesis results, stated against the register

| Hypothesis | Result | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| H1 — Interpretation is separable from experience | **SUPPORTED, narrowly** | Mayer & Fuhrmann 2022: within-person reported label shift. Instruments (USEQ, PAGE-R-II) code experience and interpretation as separate options. |
| H2 — The remembered experience itself stayed constant | **NOT ESTABLISHED** | Both datapoints are retrospective reports collected in one session. The study cannot show the remembered experience was unchanged. |
| H3 — The shift runs toward anomalous interpretations | **REVERSED** | Modal movement is toward the mundane label (sleep paralysis 10.5% → 63%), in a sample recruited through sleep-paralysis interest. A small counterflow runs the other way (~7% alien, ~7% apparition toward). |
| H4 — Apparitions and entity encounters share a generating mechanism | **NOT SUPPORTED** | Empty content axis; no coded comparand. |
| H5 — Apparitions are tableaus, contact reports are sequences | **UNTESTED, and untestable in the 1894 census** | Census codes no duration field. Hypothesis remains open, not negative. |
| H6 — Corroboration distinguishes the two classes | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, with the investigators' own disclaimer attached** | 1894 census: 95 collective visual cases, evidence beyond one witness's recollection in 43. The authors explicitly declined to treat their own numbers as a rate. |
| H7 — Non-human content dissociates the classes | **EMPTY, not zero** | Chapter XV codes recognised vs unrecognised *human* figures throughout. There is no non-human cell to compare. |
| H8 — Sleep paralysis generates entity content that culture then labels | **SUPPORTED in direction, unmeasured in magnitude on this host** | Culture-tracking content within sleep paralysis is reported across sources; no coded cross-cultural baseline exists on the host for validation. |

**Missing data is never scored as negative.** H5, H7 and the baseline for H8 are *empty*, not zero. That distinction is the module's discipline and it is load-bearing for the protocol in §5.

## 2. The four models

**Model A — shared underlying process.** No supporting measurement in this module. The one measured connection (reporting shaped by interpretation) is compatible with A but does not require it.

**Model B — distinct processes, similar experiences.** Unfalsified and unexcluded. The empty content axis means the apparent similarity was never measured at a content level in the first place.

**Model C — shared perception and cultural interpretation.** Supported at the reporting level, with the limits in §1. **Importantly, C and A are not mutually exclusive** — a generator can produce unusual percepts while culture determines the label, and this module is consistent with exactly that combination.

**Model D — heterogeneous mixture.** Survives as always, and per Ohm's standing rule it obliges us to name the residue rather than rest on its safety. The residue here: any case in which a *coded feature*, not a label, distinguishes the classes after controlling for context. None exists in the register.

## 3. What the sources actually establish

**Mayer & Fuhrmann 2022** (380 self-selected sleep-paralysis experiencers, DOI 10.31156/jaex.23534, verified via Lund OJS as well as the IGPP copy): reported interpretations differ between recalled initial understanding and current understanding. The study cannot independently establish whether the remembered experience also changed. Interpretation percentages are percentages **of sleep-paralysis experiencers**, not of abduction or apparition reports. Reading them backwards is a transposed conditional and this document does not do it.

**1894 SPR Report on the Census of Hallucinations**, Chapter XV (Proceedings of the SPR vol. 10; verified copy 22,051,479 bytes, md5 3610e113cc8fc1b66b122301e9e59c8b): 95 visual cases described as collective, evidence beyond a single witness's recollection in 43. The investigators themselves wrote that conclusions must rest on individual cases carefully scrutinised, not on alleged numbers, and noted that some supposed second percipients had no recollection of the incident. **Missing or unavailable second witnesses are not disproving witnesses** — a percipient who cannot be found did not deny the event.

**The two additional census findings** (Lewis, verified): collective visual cases skew heavily outdoor (44% vs 15% in unshared cases), where the investigators' *own* reasoning is that outdoor cases are more likely to be misidentified real people than to be genuine — the inference runs opposite to the direction a casual reader would take; and content coding throughout the chapter is recognised vs unrecognised **human** figures, with no non-human, animal or indeterminate category and no duration field (durations appear only in prose).

## 4. M7, scored as two propositions

The cross-reference mechanism M7 conflates two claims and they are held apart here:

- **M7a — ordinary cognitive processes shape how experiences are interpreted and remembered.** Independently supported by this module. This is what H1 measures.
- **M7b — the subconscious translates experience originating from an external nonordinary reality.** **EVIDENCE-IMMUNE.** Not merely unverified: formulated so that no observation could disconfirm it, because any experience can be described as translated from a source we cannot access. Evidence-immunity is a different defect from evidence-poverty and a reader must not be able to take M7a's support as support for M7b.

**Retained distinction:** "no relevant independent test within this register" is not "no independent source exists anywhere." Four of the seven mechanisms having no leverage in our material is a statement about our material.

## 5. Proposed comparison protocol — REVISED to the within-population design

**Superseding note.** An earlier version of this section proposed a cross-literature comparison (apparition cases coded against sleep-paralysis controls). **Ohm's audit showed that design has no discriminating power and it is withdrawn.** Reason, stated plainly: if the coded features differ, the difference is guaranteed by the fact that the arms were drawn from different literatures, selected by different people, about different situations; if they match, the result is the shared-vocabulary finding already predicted. Either outcome has its interpretation fixed before the data arrive — the exact failure mode this investigation spent a week diagnosing in other work. The original text is not reproduced here; it is superseded, not preserved, because it was never published and carrying it forward would imply it was a defensible option.

### 5.1 The question

Do free sensory descriptions of unusual experiences cluster by the reporter's own interpretation, or by characteristics of the episode independent of that interpretation?

### 5.2 Design

**Within-population only.** Recruit people who report sleep-paralysis episodes — one population, one sampling frame, no cross-literature comparison.

**Order of collection is the design:**

1. **Sensory description first.** Respondents describe what was seen, heard, felt — with **no invitation to characterise or name** what it was. The prompt must not contain the words ghost, spirit, alien, entity, presence, hallucination, or sleep paralysis.
2. **Interpretation second**, collected only after the description is locked: what do you think it was?
3. **Prior exposure recorded** as a covariate — whether they knew the term sleep paralysis before the episode, where they read about it, and whether they had already formed an account of the episode before the study.

**Coding:** descriptions are coded blind to the respondent's stated interpretation and blind to category-restoring cues (term use, investigator names, publication source). The primary measure is whether a coder who sees only the sensory description can recover the respondent's stated interpretation better than chance.

### 5.2b Prompt construction — fixed after audit

The word-ban alone is insufficient. Narration organises around what the thing *was* even when the forbidden nouns are absent, so a prompt that says "describe the experience" reintroduces cueing through its shape rather than its vocabulary.

**Required wording structure: separate sensory-modality questions, no narrative whole.**

- "What did you see?" — recorded separately.
- "What did you hear?" — separately.
- "What did you physically feel?" (pressure, touch, temperature, movement) — separately.
- "Was anything touching you?" — separately.

**Forbidden in the description block:** any instruction to describe, narrate, recount or explain the episode as a whole; any reference to what it was, who it was, or what it seemed to be; and the terms ghost, spirit, alien, entity, presence, hallucination, sleep paralysis, demon, apparition, vision, abduction, encounter.

Each modality is answered before the next is displayed, so no answer is shaped by seeing the previous one.

### 5.3 Pre-registered predictions — five outcomes

**Axis 1 is whether any recoverable structure exists; axis 2 is which structure. They are separate and the earlier four-cell version conflated them.**

- **P1 — shared experience-generation with culture on top (Model A).** Descriptions cluster by episode characteristics, independent of interpretation. A blind coder does **not** recover the stated label above chance. Culture supplies the name, not the percept.
- **P2 — interpretation shaping what is perceived and recalled (Model C).** A blind coder **does** recover the stated label above chance. Interpretation is present in the description, not only in the label.
- **P3 — partial recovery (A+C jointly).** Better than chance but well below the ceiling the label categories permit; some structure is physiological, some is interpretative. Stated as partial **recovery**, not partial clustering, since clustering is the dimmer of the two axes.
- **P4 — no recoverable structure at all.** This is the true null and it is **not** P3. No coding scheme separates the descriptions by interpretation. Honest reading: **insufficient evidence**, reported as such. It must never be written up as "no shared mechanism" or as evidence against any generating process.
- **P5 — degenerate instrumentation.** Structure appears but tracks something other than interpretation (recency, prior exposure, education, vocabulary size), detected by covariate analysis. Treated as a measurement failure, not a finding.

### 5.3b Numeric pre-registration

**Chance baseline.** Interpretation categories k = 6 (supernatural entity / alien or extraterrestrial / sleep-related or medical / dream-related / stress or psychological / other-or-unclassifiable), so **chance accuracy = 1/6 ≈ 16.7%** for a single-answer recovery. If the coder is permitted a ranked output, chance is computed for the ranking procedure in advance, not after.

**Minimum detectable effect.** At **n = 120** coded descriptions and 6 categories, one-versus-chance binomial testing at α = 0.05 (two-tailed) detects a true recovery rate of **≥ 27%** with power ≈ 0.80. This is the smallest effect worth calling structure.

**Sample size.** n = 120 analysable descriptions, recruited to 200 to absorb non-response and untranscribable or otherwise unusable entries. Recruitment target is fixed here, not adjusted after seeing the first data.

**Decision rules, fixed in advance.**

- P2 declared if recovery ≥ 27% and the lower bound of the 95% CI exceeds 16.7%.
- P1 declared if recovery's CI contains or falls below 16.7% **and** the study met n.
- P4 declared only if the study met n **and** covariate analysis found no structure. If n was not met, the result is P4-pending, i.e. **uninterpretable** — never reported as a null.
- P3 declared if recovery sits between the P2 threshold and chance with a CI excluding chance.

This is the amendment Ohm required and its absence was the AWARE-II defect reproduced inside our own protocol: a null is indistinguishable from insufficient power unless every one of these numbers is fixed before scoring.

### 5.4 What this design can and cannot claim

**Can:** whether interpretation is recoverable from sensory-only description in a population where the label is salient — a measurable quantity with a pre-declared chance floor and threshold, not a plausibility argument.

**Cannot:** measure naive perception. Anyone recruited through sleep-paralysis interest has almost certainly met the term before the study, so the instrument recovers *interpretation stability under recall*, not perception prior to interpretation. Ohm's point; a boundary on the claim rather than a fixable defect.

**Cannot:** speak to apparition or entity-contact cases. Within-population by construction; it answers a question about labelling in general, not the two categories that started the module. Stated at the top of this section deliberately.

**Cannot:** establish that any generating process is absent. A P4 result means the coding scheme found nothing, not that nothing exists.
### 5.5 Feasibility — stated, not assumed

| Requirement | Status |
|---|---|
| A runnable recruitment frame for sleep-paralysis experiencers | **UNKNOWN** — no capability established on this host or under current authorisation |
| Ethics/consent pathway for human respondents | **NOT ESTABLISHED** |
| Coded cross-cultural baseline for external validation | **NOT HELD** — and see §5.6 |
| Coding scheme with acceptable inter-rater reliability | **NOT BUILT** — reliability must be measured before scoring, not after |

**Therefore: this protocol is not currently runnable.** It is published as a proposal with its feasibility explicitly unresolved. A preregistration for a study that cannot be executed is the same class of artifact as a matrix built on prose, and both of us have said so.

### 5.6 Correction to "does not exist"

§6 and the earlier text describe several data needs as absent. **That is a statement about this host's holdings, not about the literatures.** A coded cross-cultural sleep-paralysis baseline very likely exists somewhere and is not here. Every such entry should read "not currently available to us." — Ohm's correction, adopted.

### 5.7 The claim that constrains nothing

Recorded here because it bears on how this module should be cited: the strongest surviving claim — that unusual-experience reporting is shaped by interpretation processes — **constrains no hypothesis and rules out nothing.** A finding that survives every audit because it denies nothing is not a discovery; it is a description of the instrument. Any later reader who takes it as having settled something is misreading it, and the report should say so where the claim is made.
## 6. Unresolved

2. The entity-contact arm has no primary sources and no verified access route.
3. The 1894 census gives one corroboration denominator with the investigators' disclaimer; it gives no content axis and no duration field, so two of the module's hypotheses have no 19th-century test.

## 7. Conclusion

**The strongest currently supported connection is between unusual-experience *reporting* and *interpretation* processes.** It is compatible with multiple experience-generating mechanisms, it establishes no external source, and it eliminates none.

That claim is deliberately the smallest one available. Its value is that it is measured, bounded, and cannot be read larger than it is — which in this literature is rarer than it sounds. A claim compatible with every generating mechanism is a description of *reporting*, not a discovery about *experience*, and this module should be read as exactly that.

**Confidence:** HIGH that interpretation is separable from experience at the reporting level. HIGH that the 1894 census disclaims its own corroboration figure. MODERATE that the label shift is direction-driven by sampling rather than by time. LOW on anything about the experiences themselves. **The proposed comparison is feasible in principle and not feasible on current holdings.**
