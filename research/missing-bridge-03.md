SPRO supplies a second, independent family of denominators: Gauld and Cornell (1979) with **500 cases** and effect frequencies (small-object movement 64%, rapping 48%, large-object movement 36%, phantom 29%, voice 26%), Carrington and Fodor (1951) with 375, Bozzano with 158 poltergeist cases drawn from a 520-case haunting collection, Puls (1900) with 70, Flammarion (1923) with 100, Thurston with 36.

### 1.2 Inclusion criteria (fixed before selection)

A case enters the register only if: (1) it is listed in Appendix A of D2024 or named with a source in SPRO; (2) it carries at least one citation to a primary or near-primary record; (3) at least one operator-specified field can be populated from text I hold.

### 1.3 Exclusion criteria (fixed before selection)

Excluded: cases named only as passing illustrations with no citation; cases whose sole record is a compilation with no traceable earlier source. **No case is excluded for being fraudulent, contested, or debunked.** Fraud and contested cases are retained and coded E-4, because dropping them would reproduce the selection error under examination.

### 1.4 Time period

No imposed lower bound. The frame spans 1526 (Lyons) to 2021 (Mexico City). Undated cases retain a missing date rather than a reconstructed one.

### 1.5 What this frame cannot do

It cannot produce a population base rate. It produces a **literature base rate** — a rate within cases that were reported, investigated and published. That distinction is developed in §6 and is the central limitation of this document.

### 3.2 The stress field — the single most important coding result

There are only two possible readings, and they are materially different:

1. **Coding gap** — stress is discussed in the primary source and my register does not capture it. That is my failure and repairable by reading the originals.
2. **Record gap** — the primary sources assert stress without documenting it independently of the case. That is a finding about the literature.

I can distinguish these only by reading primary accounts, which I have not done. **What can be said now:** the living-agent / RSPK model rests on a variable — emotional strain — that is not independently measured anywhere in the accessible secondary literature, and is plausibly inferred from the very phenomena it is invoked to explain. ## 4. Sampling bias — the author's own disclosure

D2024 states its selection mechanism verbatim: cases were chosen by "the level of testimonial and detail of the case... and their historical position (we have favored the most recent cases)." Appendix B quantifies it: **60% of the selected cases are less than a century old, against 43% in the global database.**

This is an unusually honest disclosure and it is decisive for the central question. A list selected on *detail sufficient to illustrate a category* cannot supply category frequencies. The 105-case list is an illustration set, and the agent figures quoted from the 906-case set inherit whatever selection produced the repository in the first place.

**Denominator inconsistency, flagged by Lewis:** the paper gives the repository as "about 1250" in one place and "1270" in another — a roughly 20-case gap in the stated denominator before any question of the 906/105 split arises. Both figures appear in the same publication.

**Geography and recency bias stack.** With 60% of selected cases under a century old and a European weighting in the cited list, the sample over-represents the period and region in which investigators were publishing — which is also the period and region in which the living-agent model was being promoted.

## 5. Does focal-person localisation survive the sampling correction?

**Inside the literature: yes, as a majority pattern.** D2024 codes "one agent can be clearly identified with local influence — when they are outside the place, nothing happened" as its leading category across 906 pheno-coded records.

**As a claim about the phenomenon: no, and it cannot be, on this evidence.** Three reasons, in order of strength:

1. **The repository is a set of written-up cases.** A case enters it because someone investigated and published. Whether a focal person existed plausibly affects whether the case was written up at all, so the denominator rules out "nobody looked" and does not address "nobody was there." — Ohm's objection, adopted here in full.
2. **The stress variable is missing from the instrument** (§3.2). Ohm's text search for "stress" across the Dullin full text returned nothing, meaning the 906 coded records do not contain a stress field at all. If true, the correlation between strain and phenomena was never measured, and the causal story has no test inside this corpus.
3. **Agentless cases were answered at zero by construction** (§3.2). Where no focal person is recorded, no stress question was asked or could be. Comparing owner-linked to agentless cases on stress compares two groups measured at zero because the instrument had no field.

**The defensible statement:** focal-person localisation is a majority pattern **within published poltergeist case reports**, with a genuine minority coded as agentless or place-based (New Jersey South 1973, Croydon King's Cellar, Compiègne 2011, Borley 1927, plus the sources' own "rare" qualifier, plus Stevenson 1972: "Neither always. Some poltergeists are living and others are dead"). Whether it is a majority pattern in the phenomenon cannot be determined from literature built by investigators testing the agent hypothesis.

## 6. Counterexamples and contradictions

**The strongest counterexamples to a living-agent model are inside the corpus, not imported from scepticism:**

- **New Jersey South, 1973** — 24 witnesses; alarm system replaced three times and still triggering with no one inside and no infraction detected. No focal person.
- **Croydon King's Cellar, 1960** — the same disturbances across three successive managers. A person-linked model predicts cessation on departure; the phenomena persisted across personnel changes, which is a place-linked signature.
- **Compiègne, 2011** — sounds of children moving furniture on a floor confirmed locked, empty and long unused, heard by a general assembly. Place-linked, no agent.
- **Bremen 1965** — the *opposite* difficulty: breakages followed the apprentice to a new workplace. Pro-agent, but requires that the effect track the person rather than the building.

**The Bremen and Croydon pair is the most useful contradiction in the register.** They pull in opposite directions on the same question — whether the effect follows the person or the place — and both are cited approvingly in the same literature. A model that must accommodate both needs a mechanism with two very different localisation behaviours.

**Contested and fraudulent cases (7 of 38, 18%):** Shropshire 1884, Ipswich 1888, Waterford 1891, Netherfield, Columbus, Hydesville 1848, Borley 1927. Retained rather than dropped, per the exclusion criteria in §1.3.

## 7. Seth in comparison — textual parallel, not causation

**What Seth claims (Seth corpus, Phase One §2):** emotional intensity spontaneously forms electromagnetic units; above a threshold these emerge into matter via coordinate points; the emergence is explicitly neutral with respect to content; coordinated points are located rather than abstract.

**Where the parallel genuinely holds:**

- Both place a *person-state* at the origin of the effect rather than an external agent. Seth's intensity has an owner; RSPK's discharge has an agent. That is a real structural resemblance, not a verbal one.
- Both predict that the effect tracks the person more than the location — which is exactly the Bremen finding, and the opposite of Croydon and Compiègne.

**Where it fails:**

- **The agentless cases.** Seth's mechanism has no slot for effects with no owner. New Jersey South, Croydon and Compiègne are structurally impossible under it. The RSPK model has the same problem; Stevenson and Gauld and Cornell at least admit it rather than explaining it away.
- **Neutrality versus pattern.** Seth's emergence is content-neutral. Poltergeist reports are patterned — moving objects, not generating matter — and often suggest intent. A neutral process should produce a different distribution of effects than the one recorded.
- **No independent measurement of intensity.** The same defect as the RSPK stress variable, in a stronger form: Seth's intensity has no operational definition separable from its outcome.

**Conclusion:** a resemblance in shape, with a shared and fatal gap — neither model can explain the agentless minority, and neither operationalises its input variable independently of its output. The Seth corpus does not add evidential weight to the poltergeist literature, and the poltergeist literature does not corroborate Seth.

## 8. Findings that survive, and findings that do not

**Survive:**

1. The focal-person pattern is a majority in the published literature, with a stated denominator and an explicitly disclosed selection mechanism.
2. A real minority of cases in the same literature is agentless or place-based, admitted by the sources themselves.
3. 18% of accessible cases are fraudulent or seriously contested.
4. The stress variable is undocumented — either absent from the instrument entirely (Ohm) or unrecorded in my register — and in neither case does it support a causal role.
5. Instrumentation in the strong cases excludes one specific ordinary cause each, and never the full set. No case in the register is E-1.

**Do not survive:**

1. The claim that focal localisation is an artefact of famous-case selection — the majority pattern has a denominator. My Phase Two audit was right about my sampling, wrong about the field's.
2. The claim that focal localisation is a property of the phenomenon — the denominator is a literature denominator and cannot bear population weight.
3. The claim that stress drives poltergeist phenomena — untestable against this corpus, not merely unproven.
4. The claim that Seth's mechanism accounts for poltergeist data — it fails on the agentless minority and on content neutrality.

## 9. What would actually advance this

1. **The coding instrument or codebook.** Whether a stress field exists settles whether stress was ever measured. Ohm's null text search is strong evidence but not the instrument itself.
2. **Primary texts for the eight cases with a named focal person.** This separates coding gap from record gap at source level.
3. **Appendix B variable list** — whether the pheno-coding includes a strain dimension applicable to agentless cases.
4. **A literature-independent denominator.** None is currently constructible. Any method of estimating unreported cases would change the question entirely.

### C-1. The fraud percentage was computed from a denominator that did not include the cases

**Original claim (§3.1):** "Seven of 38 accessible cases are fraudulent or seriously contested — 18% of the accessible register," presented as a floor under the field's fraud rate.

**Why it is wrong:** the seven were listed in prose from the SPR overview as fraud-discovered cases. Six of them — Shropshire, Ipswich, Waterford, Netherfield, Columbus, Hydesville — were never entered as numbered rows in the register. The numbered register has 38 rows and exactly one of those rows (Borley, #17) is contested. The six were therefore never in the denominator that the 18% was calculated against.

**Corrected figures:**

| Set | Count |
|---|---|
| Numbered register rows | 38 |
| Rows coded contested/discredited (E-4) | 1 (Borley) |
| Fraud-discovered cases named by SPRO but not coded as rows | 5 (Shropshire, Ipswich, Waterford, Netherfield, Columbus) |
| Retrospectively admitted fraud, named but not coded | 1 (Hydesville 1848) |

**Withdrawn:** the 18% figure, and **all** of the accompanying claim that it establishes a floor under the field's fraud rate. A count of cases named in a summary article, never entered into the register, is not a sample and supports no rate at all.

**What survives:** the fraud cases exist, are real, total at least six in the literature surveyed, and were deliberately not excluded — the *exclusion policy* in §1.3 stands. Only the arithmetic and the inference drawn from it are withdrawn.

### C-2. "No focal person" conflates two different things

**Original claim (§3, rows 15, 16, 17, 18; §5 reason 3):** cases marked **NONE IDENTIFIED** or **NEITHER**, treated as evidence that no focal person existed.

**Why it is wrong:** a report with no identified focal person is not evidence that no focal person existed. It is evidence that no focal person was identified — by that investigator, in that report, with those methods. Conflating the two is an absence-of-evidence error of exactly the kind this investigation exists to catch.

**Reclassification required, and now adopted:** place-localisation and absence-of-focal-person are separate categories.

| Case | Reported localisation | Independently demonstrated absence of a focal person? |
|---|---|---|
| New Jersey South 1973 | Alarm triggered with nobody present, 24 witnesses | **No.** Nobody present at trigger time ≠ no focal person existed. The case may simply be unbounded in time. |
| Croydon King's Cellar 1960 | Persisted across three successive managers | **No.** Persistence across personnel is consistent with either a place-link or an unidentified agent; it does not demonstrate agentlessness. |
| Compiègne 2011 | Floor confirmed empty; heard by assembly | **No.** The floor was empty; the building was not, and the listeners were in it. |
| Borley 1927 | Unoccupied at observation | **No.** Contested case; treated as E-4 regardless. |

**Revised §5 reason 3:** the agentless minority is a minority of *reports that did not identify an agent*, which is not the same set as *events with no agent*. The claim that a genuine agentless class exists, resting on Stevenson 1972 and Gauld and Cornell 1979, is theirs and is reported as theirs — I have not verified it and cannot from this material.

### C-3. Absence of a stress field is not proof stress was never assessed

**Original claim (§3.2):** stress undocumented in 0 of 38, presented as a finding about the literature with a binary split between coding gap and record gap.

**Why it needs narrowing:** Ohm's reading of line 1716 establishes that the 1-to-10 scale rates *testimonial quality*, not emotional state, and that the only emotional language in the paper is definitional (1258) and advisory (1716). The further finding is that Appendix B is supporting demographic figures, not a variable list — so no codebook is published. The correct statement is therefore **not** "the instrument has no emotional dimension" but **"the published paper does not reveal one, and no codebook is published to confirm or deny a wider instrument."**

**What survives, and it is the important part:** the RSPK causal story, as published, rests on a variable that appears nowhere in the published coding and for which no assessment instrument is disclosed. That is a statement about what the literature shows, not about what the investigators did.

### C-4. Scope warning adopted

Nothing in this document licenses the inference that emotional intensity goes unmeasured in other paranormal domains, nor that NDEs produce no reported physical effects. Both were flagged as overreaches and both are now explicitly out of scope. The comparative work treats them as not-asked cells pending sources.
