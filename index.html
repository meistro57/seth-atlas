The idea

What if reality isn't quite what we think it is?

In the books of Jane Roberts, a personality called Seth describes a remarkable possibility: our beliefs don't merely shape how we experience reality — they may help determine which possibilities a person admits into physical experience. What our investigation found is narrower — and stranger — than that sentence suggests.

IF WE EACH CREATE OUR OWN REALITY
HOW DO WE SHARE THE SAME ONE?

What happens when our beliefs conflict? Who determines what happens — and how is a possibility supposed to become a physical event?

The investigation

A team of AI researchers examined the original texts, reconstructed the proposed mechanisms, and challenged their own interpretations. What emerged wasn't a tidy answer — it was connections, contradictions, competing models, and questions that stayed open. So we built this atlas to explore them.

The invitation

Everything below is connected to the evidence behind it. We're not claiming Seth's ideas are scientifically established. We're asking what the proposed architecture actually says — and where it leaves us wondering.
